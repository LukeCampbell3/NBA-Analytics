Inside the Formula 1 ledger

How the F1 Board Works

A chronological pre-race model, a consensus outright market, and a deliberately cautious shadow publication policy.

Prediction Target

The system estimates each driver’s probability of winning, finishing on the podium, and finishing in the top six at the next Grand Prix. The market edge board compares only race-winner probabilities with race-winner outright prices, keeping the first version auditable.

Features Available Before Lights Out

  • Starting-grid position and a separate flag indicating whether the grid is known.
  • Rolling driver finishes, wins, podiums, starts, and DNF rate.
  • Rolling constructor average finish, recent form, and win rate.
  • Driver history at the circuit plus current-season points share and standing.

Historical features are updated only after each race row has been created, preventing the result being predicted from leaking into its inputs.

Model and Evaluation

Three regularized logistic models estimate win, podium, and top-six outcomes. Field probabilities are normalized to the number of available finishing slots. The final 20% of races are held out chronologically and never shuffled into training; the board reports Brier scores, winner log loss, and top-ranked winner accuracy.

Chronological Holdout

The holdout is the final 20% of historical races in time order. It is model evidence, not a profit claim.

Prospective Tracker

Scheduled boards are archived before each race and graded after official results become available.

Odds and Publication Controls

Credential-free Polymarket and Kalshi public APIs supply executable YES asks. The board averages one observation per exchange for consensus and uses the lowest live ask as the best available price.

Missing or incomplete markets fail closed: model probabilities may be shown, but no edge is invented. Every play is labeled research-only, and staking remains disabled until enough prospective race snapshots have been graded.
